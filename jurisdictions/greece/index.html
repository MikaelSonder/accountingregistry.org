Executive Summary

Accounting in Greece is the structured financial recording and reporting function through which a business documents transactions, preserves accounting evidence and prepares annual financial statements in an organised legal and operational environment. In practice, the function is not limited to bookkeeping entries, because it also includes classification discipline, supporting documentation, periodisation logic and year-end reporting structure.

Operationally, Greek accounting typically begins with the continuous capture of business events such as sales, purchases, payroll-related inputs, bank transactions, VAT-relevant movements and other tax-facing activity. These events must be organised into records that can later support internal control, statutory reporting and management understanding.

The Greek environment places importance on orderly books, annual reporting under Law 4308/2014 on Greek Accounting Standards, tax administration through AADE and company-publicity filing through GEMI where applicable. As a result, accounting in Greece is not only a bookkeeping discipline, but also a structured financial reporting, tax-compliance and document-publication function.

Cross-border relevance is substantial where Greek entities belong to foreign-owned groups, where foreign investors operate through Greek companies or where local accounting output must support wider international reporting expectations alongside Greek statutory obligations.

Purpose

The purpose of accounting in Greece is to create a reliable, traceable and legally usable financial record of business activity. It exists to ensure that transactions can be recorded, documented, reviewed and translated into annual financial statements suitable for statutory and operational use.

In practical business terms, the function supports legal compliance, management visibility, internal control, year-end readiness, tax-facing reporting and document-publication readiness where filing duties arise.

Primary Outcome

A coherent accounting position in Greece, including orderly bookkeeping records, documented financial events, annual financial statements prepared under the applicable framework and correct handling of filing and retention obligations.

FAQ
Is accounting in Greece only about bookkeeping? No. It also includes annual financial statements, tax-facing reporting, filing or publication duties and year-end accounting responsibilities.
Where are annual financial statements filed in Greece? Relevant companies file annual financial statements through GEMI, the General Commercial Register, as part of the Greek company-publicity environment.
How long are accounting records generally retained in Greece? Accounting records are generally retained for at least five years from the balance sheet date or end of the relevant financial year, subject to longer periods under other applicable legislation.
Can foreign-owned companies in Greece need local accounting compliance? Yes. A Greek entity operating within an international group still needs local accounting organisation and reporting discipline in Greece.

Practical Guidance

A business entering or operating in Greece should first establish who is responsible for the bookkeeping chain, how supporting documents are captured and how annual closing, tax-facing reporting and filing routines are controlled. In the Greek environment, accounting quality depends heavily on orderly records, correct timing and disciplined handling of reporting and publication steps.

Cross-border businesses should also determine early whether Greek local outputs must feed foreign management, group reporting or investor-facing communication. If so, the accounting structure should be organised so that Greek statutory expectations and international reporting needs can operate together without conflict.
