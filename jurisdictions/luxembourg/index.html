Executive Summary

Accounting in Luxembourg is the structured financial recording and reporting function through which a business documents transactions, preserves accounting evidence and prepares annual accounts in an organised legal and operational environment. In practice, the function is not limited to bookkeeping entries, because it also includes classification discipline, supporting documentation, periodisation logic and year-end reporting structure.

Operationally, Luxembourg accounting usually begins with continuous bookkeeping of sales, purchases, payroll-related items, bank movements, tax-relevant activity and other business events. These records support annual accounts, internal control and broader financial reliability.

The Luxembourg framework places strong emphasis on orderly books, the standard chart of accounts, annual accounts, electronic preparation through the eCDF platform where applicable, filing with the Trade and Companies Register and structured record-retention obligations. As a result, accounting in Luxembourg is not only a bookkeeping discipline, but also a structured year-end reporting, electronic filing and archive-management function.

Cross-border relevance is substantial where Luxembourg entities belong to foreign-owned groups, form part of international holding or financing structures, or need local accounting output to support wider reporting expectations alongside Luxembourg statutory obligations.

Purpose

The purpose of accounting in Luxembourg is to create a reliable, traceable and legally usable financial record of business activity. It exists to ensure that transactions can be recorded, documented, reviewed and translated into annual accounts suitable for statutory and operational use.

In practical business terms, the function supports legal compliance, management visibility, internal control, year-end readiness and electronic filing readiness where RCS filing duties arise.

Primary Outcome

A coherent accounting position in Luxembourg, including orderly bookkeeping records, documented financial events, annual accounts prepared under the applicable framework and correct handling of filing and retention obligations.

Timeline
Initial Setup Usually arises at or near incorporation, first transactions or the start of Luxembourg operations.
Ongoing Activity Accounting is a recurring function based on continuous bookkeeping and regular reconciliation discipline.
Year-End Stage Annual accounts are prepared as part of the recurring closing cycle for the entity.
Approval and Filing Stage Annual accounts are generally approved within six months after financial year-end and filed with the RCS within one month after approval, resulting in a general filing timetable of seven months after year-end.
Retention Stage Accounting documents and records are generally kept for ten years; in the event of liquidation, the general retention period may be five years. The place of preservation must be Luxembourg.

FAQ
Is accounting in Luxembourg only about bookkeeping? No. It also includes annual accounts, PCN classification, eCDF preparation, RCS filing, record retention and year-end accounting responsibilities.
Where are annual accounts filed in Luxembourg? Annual accounts are filed electronically with the Trade and Companies Register, RCS, managed by Luxembourg Business Registers, generally after eCDF preparation where applicable.
How long are accounting records generally retained in Luxembourg? Accounting documents and records are generally retained for ten years; in the event of liquidation, the general retention period may be five years. The place of preservation must be Luxembourg.
Can foreign-owned companies in Luxembourg need local accounting compliance? Yes. A Luxembourg entity operating within an international group still needs local accounting organisation and reporting discipline in Luxembourg.

Practical Guidance

A business entering or operating in Luxembourg should first establish who is responsible for the bookkeeping chain, how supporting documents are captured and how year-end closing, eCDF preparation and RCS filing routines are controlled. In the Luxembourg environment, accounting quality depends heavily on orderly records, correct timing and disciplined handling of approval and electronic filing steps.

Cross-border businesses should also determine early whether Luxembourg local outputs must feed foreign management, group reporting or investor-facing communication. If so, the accounting structure should be organised so that Luxembourg statutory expectations and international reporting needs can operate together without conflict.
