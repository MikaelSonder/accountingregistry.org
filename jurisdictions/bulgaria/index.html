Executive Summary

Accounting in Bulgaria is the structured financial recording and reporting function through which a business documents transactions, preserves accounting evidence and prepares annual financial statements in an organised legal and operational environment. In practice, the function is not limited to bookkeeping entries, because it also includes classification discipline, supporting documentation, periodisation logic and year-end reporting structure.

Operationally, Bulgarian accounting usually begins with continuous bookkeeping of sales, purchases, payroll-related items, tax-relevant movements and other business events. These records support annual financial statements, internal control and broader financial reliability.

The Bulgarian framework places strong emphasis on orderly books, annual reporting under the Accountancy Act, publication through the Commercial Register and structured record-retention obligations. As a result, accounting in Bulgaria is not only a bookkeeping discipline, but also a structured year-end reporting, document-publication and archive-management function.

Cross-border relevance is substantial where Bulgarian entities belong to foreign-owned groups, where foreign investors operate through Bulgarian companies or where local accounting output must support wider international reporting expectations alongside Bulgarian statutory obligations.

Purpose

The purpose of accounting in Bulgaria is to create a reliable, traceable and legally usable financial record of business activity. It exists to ensure that transactions can be recorded, documented, reviewed and translated into annual financial statements suitable for statutory and operational use.

In practical business terms, the function supports legal compliance, management visibility, internal control, year-end readiness and document-publication readiness where filing duties arise.

Primary Outcome

A coherent accounting position in Bulgaria, including orderly bookkeeping records, documented financial events, annual financial statements prepared under the applicable framework and correct handling of publication and retention obligations.

Timeline

Year-End Stage Annual financial statements are prepared as part of the recurring closing cycle for the entity.

FAQ
Is accounting in Bulgaria only about bookkeeping? No. It also includes annual financial statements, publication duties, reporting obligations and year-end accounting responsibilities.
Where are annual financial statements filed in Bulgaria? Traders publish annual financial statements in the Commercial Register maintained by the Registry Agency, while separate statistical information is filed with the National Statistical Institute where applicable.
How long are accounting records generally retained in Bulgaria? Accounting ledgers and financial statements, including documents for tax control and audit, are generally retained for ten years; payroll records for fifty years; and other accounting-information carriers for three years.
Can foreign-owned companies in Bulgaria need local accounting compliance? Yes. A Bulgarian entity operating within an international group still needs local accounting organisation and reporting discipline in Bulgaria.

Practical Guidance

A business entering or operating in Bulgaria should first establish who is responsible for the bookkeeping chain, how supporting documents are captured and how year-end closing and Commercial Register publication routines are controlled. In the Bulgarian environment, accounting quality depends heavily on orderly records, correct timing and disciplined handling of approval and reporting steps.

Cross-border businesses should also determine early whether Bulgarian local outputs must feed foreign management, group reporting or investor-facing communication. If so, the accounting structure should be organised so that Bulgarian statutory expectations and international reporting needs can operate together without conflict.
